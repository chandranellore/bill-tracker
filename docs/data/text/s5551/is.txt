                                 A BILL


 
 To promote United States and allied energy and mineral security, and 
                          for other purposes.

    Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the 
United States of America in Congress assembled,

SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

    This Act may be cited as the ``Critical Mineral Executive 
Coordination Act of 2026''.

              TITLE I--FOREIGN SERVICE INSTITUTE TRAINING

SEC. 101. FINDINGS AND DECLARATIONS.

    Congress makes the following findings and declarations:
            (1) Critical minerals and energy resources are essential to 
        United States national security, economic competitiveness, and 
        technological leadership.
            (2) According to the 2025 National Security Strategy, ``We 
        must re-secure our own independent and reliable access to the 
        goods we need to defend ourselves and preserve our way of life. 
        This will require expanding American access to critical 
        minerals and materials while countering predatory economic 
        practices.''.
            (3) Foreign Service officers play a key role in advancing 
        United States foreign policy, commercial interests, and energy 
        security objectives and receive specialized training, when 
        necessary, to carry out their job functions.
            (4) Knowledge of global energy and critical mineral supply 
        chains, including vulnerabilities posed by adversarial actors, 
        is essential for effective diplomatic engagement and mission 
        planning.
            (5) The Foreign Service Institute (FSI) is the principal 
        training institution for United States Foreign Service 
        officers, and its programs must include emerging strategic 
        areas relevant to United States interests.

SEC. 102. PURPOSES.

    The purposes of this Act are--
            (1) to ensure that Foreign Service Officers assigned to 
        positions in which energy and critical minerals are core 
        responsibilities have completed mandatory coursework on these 
        subjects;
            (2) to ensure that officers assigned to missions overseas 
        have a practical understanding of the international policies, 
        agreements, and United States strategies concerning energy and 
        critical minerals; and
            (3) to strengthen United States diplomatic capacity to 
        address vulnerabilities in global critical mineral supply 
        chains, including those influenced by the People's Republic of 
        China or other adversaries.

SEC. 103. TRAINING PROGRAM ESTABLISHMENT.

    (a) Establishment.--The Secretary of State, in consultation with 
the Under Secretary for Economic Affairs and the Director of the George 
P. Shultz National Foreign Affairs Training Center, shall establish an 
academic program focused on global supply chains and their relation to 
strategic competition with the People's Republic of China.
    (b) Training Requirements.--The academic program established under 
subsection (a) shall be mandatory for all--
            (1) entry-level Foreign Service Officers identified as 
        Economic Officers prior to the end of the second tour in the 
        Department; and
            (2) Foreign Service Officers identified as Economic 
        Officers prior to deployment at a United States mission abroad 
        to fill a billet where the primary focus of the portfolio will 
        be critical minerals or energy.
    (c) Training Content.--The academic program established under 
subsection (a) shall include the following curriculum elements:
            (1) Instruction on international documents, treaties, and 
        agreements relevant to global energy and critical mineral 
        supply chains.
            (2) United States Government policies and strategies for 
        mitigating vulnerabilities in global supply chains.
            (3) Analysis of the effects of strategic competition, 
        including with the People's Republic of China, on United States 
        interests.
            (4) Regional-and-country-specific instruction tailored to 
        the resources, economy, and international trade relationships 
        of the receiving country.
            (5) Practical guidance for officers to advance United 
        States foreign policy objectives in diplomacy, trade 
        negotiations, multilateral forums, and crisis scenarios related 
        to supply chains.
            (6) Specialized training related to critical minerals as 
        described in section 105.

SEC. 104. CRITICAL MINERAL-SPECIFIC TRAINING.

    (a) In General.--The Secretary of State shall require specialized 
training on critical minerals for Foreign Service Officers identified 
as Economic Officers prior to deployment to United States presence 
posts in countries that--
            (1) produce, process, refine, or export significant 
        quantities of critical minerals;
            (2) host critical mineral reserves of strategic importance 
        to the United States; or
            (3) play a significant role in global critical mineral 
        supply chains, including through transportation, processing, or 
        downstream manufacturing.
    (b) Content.--The specialized training related to critical minerals 
identified under subsection (a) shall include the following curriculum 
elements:
            (1) An overview of United States critical minerals policy 
        and interagency roles.
            (2) Country-and-region-specific analysis of critical 
        mineral resources, governance, and market structures.
            (3) Geopolitical and national security implications of 
        critical mineral supply chains.
            (4) Risk factors related to supply disruption, foreign 
        ownership or control, corruption, and resource nationalism.
            (5) Tools and best practices for advancing United States 
        commercial, economic security, and strategic objectives related 
        to critical minerals through diplomatic engagement.

SEC. 105. SUPPLEMENTAL TRAINING.

    The Secretary of State shall require specialized training on risk 
assessment and mitigation strategies for Foreign Service Officers 
identified as Economic Officers prior to deployment to United States 
presence posts in countries where there exists--
            (1) a heightened risk of supply chain disruptions;
            (2) existing or probable resource conflict; or
            (3) strategic resource vulnerabilities.

SEC. 106. INFORMATION SHARING.

    The Secretary of State shall make available all academic program 
materials developed under section 104 to--
            (1) the Office of the Assistant Secretary of War for 
        Industrial Base Policy;
            (2) the Office of Strategic Capital within the Office of 
        the Under Secretary of War for Acquisition and Sustainment; and
            (3) other Federal departments and agencies with personnel 
        stationed.

SEC. 107. SUNSET.

    The training requirements established in this Act shall terminate 
on the date that is three years after the date of the enactment of this 
Act.

             TITLE II--NATIONAL SECURITY COUNCIL POSITIONS

SEC. 201. NATIONAL SECURITY COUNCIL POSITIONS ESTABLISHED.

    Section 101 of the National Security Act of 1947 (50 U.S.C. 3021) 
is amended--
            (1) by redesignating subsections (g) and (h) as subsections 
        (h) and (i), respectively; and
            (2) by inserting after subsection (f) the following new 
        subsection:
    ``(g) Special Advisor to the President for Critical Minerals and 
Supply Chains.--It is the sense of Congress that there should be within 
the staff of the Council a Special Advisor to the President for 
Critical Minerals and Supply Chains, whose position should be 
equivalent to that of a Deputy National Security Advisor within the 
Executive Office of the President. The Special Advisor should serve as 
a coordinator between the responsible departments and agencies, as 
described in the National Materials and Minerals Policy, Research, and 
Development Act (30 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.) for the development of 
domestic and international supply chains related to critical materials 
(as defined in section 7002(a)(3) of the Energy Act of 2020 (30 U.S.C. 
1606(c))) and making policy recommendations. The Special Advisor should 
serve as liaison to Congress and relevant private sector industries, as 
necessary. The Special Advisor should serve as liaison to the Under 
Secretary of State for Economic Affairs (or a designee of the Under 
Secretary). The Special Advisor should be assisted by--
            ``(1) a Deputy Special Advisor to the President for 
        Critical Minerals and Supply Chains and the Senior Director for 
        Domestic Coordination; and
            ``(2) a Deputy Special Advisor to the President for 
        Critical Minerals and Supply Chains the Senior Director for 
        International Coordination.''.
